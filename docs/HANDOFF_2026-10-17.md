## 2. Trim strategy — current design (the Tom Nash framework)

Trimming is risk management on **winners only**, never a reaction to weakness.

**Ladder** — gain measured from each symbol's own ladder base price:

| gain from base | trim |
|---|---|
| +50% | sell 10% of shares held |
| +100% | sell 20% |
| +150% | sell 30%, then **re-base** to the current price and climb again |

One rung fires per scan, lowest unfired first. A saturated ladder self-heals by re-basing.
Rung state persists in `trim_ladder_state.json`.

**Adaptive first-rung gate (implemented 2026-10-17, commit `cf0bc2e`)** — the +50% rung's
10% trim is **skipped** when the move is already parabolic. At the moment the price first
reaches +50% of the ladder base, compute `extension = close / 50-day SMA`. If
`extension >= 1.20` the rung is marked passed without selling and the position is allowed to
run; otherwise the trim fires as normal. Only the first rung is gated, and the gate re-applies
to the first rung of each fresh cycle after a re-base. If the moving average cannot be
computed, the rung fires (refusing to trim is the riskier failure).

Rationale: a parabolic +50% is usually the early stage of a multi-bagger, whereas a choppy
+50% is a rally worth harvesting — and price alone cannot tell them apart, only extension.

**Position cap: 10% of equity.** An over-cap position is trimmed back to the cap.

**Add candidates.** A position 20%+ below its 52-week high is **reported** as an ADD
candidate — weakness is a buying opportunity, not a trim. Report only; never auto-bought.

**Outright exits are never automatic.** `thesis_break_exit(symbol, reason)` is an explicit
call; the scan only surfaces candidates.

**No blanket cooldown** — the 30-day per-symbol rule was dropped (the ladder is
self-limiting; a cooldown would block legitimate successive rungs).

Sizing is `round(qty * pct, 4)` — fractional, so the trim is exactly the stated share.

## 3. Cross-validation (the research behind the gate)

Tested on 20 names over one uniform window, every strategy **armed at the same price on the
same day** (10 highest-growth stocks entered at the 2025-10-15 close; Sharesies holdings at
the first close that reached each one's own cost basis). $1,000 notional per name.

**Panel A — 10 highest-growth stocks** (AXTI, SNDK, MRNA, IOVA, TXG, KOD, LITE, MU, MXL,
VICR; +234% to +1021% over the window):

| strategy | median | mean | worst | best | beats HOLD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HOLD | 5.819 | 6.309 | 3.341 | 11.214 | 0% |
| LADDER | 4.049 | 4.212 | 3.307 | 5.999 | 0% |
| ADAPT-1.20 | 4.110 | 4.396 | 3.342 | 6.162 | 10% |
| TREND (gate +100%) | 4.595 | 5.120 | 3.406 | 9.324 | 20% |
| TRAIL-20% | 3.498 | 4.290 | 2.783 | 9.248 | 10% |

**Panel B — Sharesies holdings** (10 usable; CO2/GFI/USH skipped — price never reached
their inferred basis):

| strategy | median | mean | worst | best | beats HOLD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HOLD | 1.000 | 1.101 | 0.598 | 1.998 | 0% |
| LADDER | 1.100 | 1.261 | 0.707 | 2.477 | 60% |
| ADAPT-1.20 | 1.077 | 1.255 | 0.707 | 2.499 | 50% |
| TRAIL-20% | 1.074 | 1.197 | 0.835 | 2.328 | 60% |
| TREND (gate +100%) | 1.044 | 1.206 | 0.598 | 2.820 | 20% |

### Robust findings

1. **Nothing beat holding on the highest-growth stocks.** Caveat: Panel A is
   survivorship-biased by construction — those names were selected for having the highest
   gains, so holding is structurally guaranteed to win. The panel cannot validate or
   invalidate trimming; it only prices the cost of trimming in the best case for holding.
2. **On the actual holdings the original ladder is the strongest mechanism tested** — it
   beat holding on 6 of 10, including three names that fell (DTIL -20% became 1.115, RGTI -3%
   became 1.586, DXYZ -40% became 0.707).
3. **The rung is a regime parameter.** Performance on Panel A rises monotonically as the
   rung is raised; on Panel B it falls monotonically. Opposite directions means no single
   fixed setting is correct.
4. **The trailing stop is NOT dominated — it is roughly level with the ladder.** It beats
   the ladder on the winners panel (4.29x vs 4.21x mean) and trails it on the holdings
   (1.20x vs 1.26x). Do not add one — but because it is not better where it matters, not
   because it is broadly bad.
5. **The "gate" is essential.** A trend rule without the "after a long gain" condition
   liquidated positions early and lost $501 on the growth names.

### A recommendation that was tested and REFUTED

Raising the first rung outright to +100% gained more on Panel A (+0.596) but cost -0.074 on
Panel B and cut "beats HOLD" there from 60% to 30%. **Rejected** — it would have made the
actual book worse. Recorded so it is not re-proposed from memory.

## 4. How the strategy evolves — a standing principle

The trim ladder is not a fixed artefact. It is a hypothesis re-tested against data and
revised when the evidence is good enough. Four rules govern any change:

1. **A change must be tested, not asserted** — backtested on the same names under the same
   arms before adoption.
2. **A change must improve one regime without damaging another** — the rung is a regime
   parameter; a variant that helps only one panel is a trade, not an improvement, and is
   adopted only when the cost on the other panel is measurably zero.
3. **Refutations are kept, not discarded** — recorded with their numbers.
4. **Code follows evidence, not the reverse** — implemented only after surviving rules 1-3,
   then pinned by tests so a later refactor cannot silently undo it.
