## HIS SYSTEM — the part that matters more than the stock list

This is consistent across every video and it is the actual product he sells:

**The F/P quadrant.** Split the market into fundamentals (F) and price (P). The two quadrants you
never want are "fundamentals falling, price falling" and "fundamentals falling, price rising". The
"good" quadrant is improving fundamentals with a flat or rising price. The "elite" quadrant — and
the title of every video is built on it — is **improving fundamentals while the price falls**.
That is the entire screen: find the divergence, then buy it.

**The DCA double-down rule.** This is his stated, mechanical entry method and it is more specific
than most creators give:

- Measure the stock against its 52-week high.
- Threshold: **10% below the 52w high for a broad market ETF**, **20% for a growth stock** (he
  names Palantir and Tesla as examples), **30% for "uber conservative"**.
- Until price is below the threshold, buy the normal monthly amount (his example: $100/month).
- The moment price crosses below the threshold, buy at **3x pace** ($300/month).
- Continue at 3x for as long as price stays below the threshold; revert to 1x when it recovers.

**The framework.** AI is a multi-year hypercycle. The money is in infrastructure, not
applications — "picks and shovels" and "the land you need to rent to dig for gold". His five
categories: compute/semiconductors (wave 1, hardware), upgrades, then software/orchestration, then
cloud/power/cooling bottlenecks, then physical AI/robotics. He repeatedly cites the Jevons
paradox (cheaper AI → more demand, not less) and argues AI is "already profitable" — his
arithmetic is $100B capex per data center generating ~$37B/yr over a 5-year life, less ~$5B/yr
opex, leaving ~$60B over 5 years.

**On bubbles.** He dismisses the dot-com comparison on numbers: NASDAQ at ~200x PE in 2000 vs
~30x now; Nvidia alone made $120B net income, more than the combined revenue of the 10 biggest
dot-com companies. He does concede one real risk — the **energy/power bottleneck** (data center
electricity quadrupling from ~400 TWh in 2024 to ~1,600 TWh by 2034; grid interconnection takes
4-10 years while AI data centers are planned in 2-3) — and argues that is a money-making
opportunity, not a reason to avoid AI.

**On Palantir specifically.** He calls it priced for perfection (104x PE at the time) but "may be
a top three company in the world", citing Rule of 40 of 145, 85% revenue growth, 60% operating
margin, 150% net dollar retention, zero debt, $8B cash, 350% operating income growth. He uses a
forward PEG of 1.2 to argue it is cheap on growth-adjusted numbers. This is the pick he has
pushed hardest and longest (19 mentions in one tracking dataset).

and AMZN are both within ~4-12% of their highs now, not 31% and 17% below. Anyone copying those
specific drawdown thresholds today would be buying at very different prices than he described.

**A note of caution before treating this as a signal.** The screener and the theme map have both
been measured and shown to have **no forward predictive power** in this system

This is a different case: Nash is a human with a stated, repeatable process and a long track
record. But his own record as tracked externally is 8 of 11 matured calls beating the market over
6 months (73%), which is good but not the "10x on every pick" impression his video titles give.
His performance claims in-video run behind the tape. His thesis is a *risk/attention* lens, not a
buy signal, and every name still has to pass the normal gates: theme cap, 8% entry cap, multiple
confirming signals, thesis review.

**The most genuinely useful thing in his recent work** is not a ticker — it is the September 8
energy-bottleneck thesis (CEG/VRT/BE) and the September 1 "cloud landlords" argument
(MSFT/GOOGL/AMZN). Both are structural arguments you can test against your own data rather than
