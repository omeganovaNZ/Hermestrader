## 1. Geopolitics

### Iran / Strait of Hormuz — the shock has no end date
15 merchant-ship attacks recorded Oct 6–14 (UKMTO), alleged naval mines now in play, and Trump reimposed the Iranian port blockade in October after a three-month ceasefire collapsed. Gulf transit is down to ~9.7m bpd from a 17m bpd baseline (Kpler). Global refinery capacity is down as much as 10% (Dallas Fed), and logistics plus insurance now account for ~a third of the cost of moving a Gulf barrel versus 3% pre-war.
- **Benefits:** XOM, CVX, NEE, GEV, CAT, BE
- **Hurts:** AIR, discretionary, rate-sensitive growth
- **Action:** Hold XOM/CVX at 8%/7%. Add GEV or CAT as the non-defense expression of the capacity squeeze.

### US-China — a truce, not a deal. Three cliffs inside six months
The Busan truce was extended to **Jan 10, 2027**. Separately, the 178 remaining Section 301 exclusions **expire Nov 10, 2026** with no extension announced, and the "30-for-30" relief framework is not yet operative (no Federal Register notice). Effective tariffs on Chinese goods still stack to ~30%.
- **Benefits:** MU, MRVL, AVGO, XOM, CAT
- **Action:** No change. Avoid new China-levered names until Nov 10 and Jan 10 resolve.

### Critical minerals — the binding constraint on AI hardware
China's expanded rare-earth measures (the 0.1% extraterritorial rule) are suspended to Nov 2026, but seven REE controls stay live and the January 2026 catalogue added compounds, silver and more. IEA: European gallium and heavy REE prices run ~5x Chinese domestic; full implementation would put $6.5tn/yr of non-China downstream production at risk.
- **Benefits:** MU (supply discipline), XOM/CVX
- **Action:** This is the structural case for memory pricing discipline. Reinforces MU over a broad semis basket.

## 2. Technology supply chains

### New chokepoints: EDA, GAA transistors, advanced packaging
Deloitte expects ≥$30bn spent by 2026 on EUV and HBM co-packaging tools, all now trade-barrier exposed; controls have spread from equipment into software, design methodologies and model weights. KPMG's 2026 survey puts supply-chain agility as the #1 priority for 45% of semis leaders, ahead of talent for the first time in three years. TSMC CoWoS carries ~85% of advanced AI chip production.
- **Benefits:** MU, MRVL, AVGO, TSM, ASML, LRCX, AMAT, KLAC, SNPS, CDNS
- **Avoid:** INTC — $627bn valuation, $3.7bn quarterly loss, external foundry revenue $293m in Q2. It's a government-sponsored policy asset (9.9% passive US stake, $8.9bn invested), not an investment.

### AI memory supercycle — sold out into 2027 at higher prices

Q1 FY27 guided to ~$61.5bn at ~86.25% margin. Management: "vast majority" of calendar-2027 HBM bit supply contracted with significant price increases, HBM to outgrow conventional DRAM through CY2028, no line of sight to market balance. FY26 capex raised above $25bn.
- **Action:** The pending MU order at $969.47 is buying a peak-cycle print. Hold what you have; add on RSI<30 dips; trim into strength. Do not size up here.

### Custom AI silicon is the second demand engine
Marvell FY26 revenue ~$8.2bn (+40%), data center $6.1bn, ~75% of total. Combined FY27+FY28 outlook raised to **$30bn** from $20bn a year ago. Custom ASIC target raised to **$12bn+ by FY2029** (from $10–11bn); custom sales doubling in FY2028; 20+ XPU/XPU-attach design wins across all four US hyperscalers; long-term path to $70–90bn by FY2031.
- **Action:** MRVL at ~62x forward earnings is the most expensive line in the book and carries single-customer concentration (Amazon + Microsoft ≈ 50%+). Hold it as the custom-silicon thesis, cap it at 15%. ALAB (Q2 2026 revenue +104% y/y, Q3 guided $540–560m) is the cleaner pure-play on rack-scale interconnect.

### The AI capex flywheel is the portfolio's largest tail risk
Hyperscaler 2026 AI capex raised to ~$730bn combined (+78% from ~$410bn in 2025), projected $1.3tn in 2027. Nvidia: ~$5.3–5.8tn cap, FY26 revenue $215.9bn, **$279bn in supply commitments**, >$540bn of circular-financing deals. Alphabet fell 7% in a session after *raising* its own capex guidance; Meta fell too.
- **Action:** NVDA stays watchlist-only. The right structural question isn't "does AI end" but "where does money survive a digestion period." Memory, connectivity and power survive; NVDA's multiple doesn't. Keep total AI-hardware exposure ≤40%.

## 3. Energy

### The power capacity squeeze is now AI's bottleneck
GE Vernova gas power backlog + slot reservations: 83 GW (end-2025) → 100 GW (Q1 2026) → **116 GW (Q2 2026)**, targeting 125 GW by year-end. Only 53 GW is firm backlog; 63 GW is paid reservations. Total backlog **$176bn**. Turbine lead times run to **2031**; combined-cycle lead times have stretched from 3.5 years (2023) to ~5, and 7 for some frames. Output: 20 GW/yr from Q3 2026, 24 GW in 2028, 30 GW in 2030.
- **Action:** **ADD GEV (5–7%).** Best non-defense expression of the AI power bottleneck, and the strongest backtest in the file (Sharpe 1.392, max drawdown −23.45%). CAT is the same trade with a **$72.1bn backlog (+92% y/y)**, $42.5bn converting within 12 months.

### Utilities re-rating from yield plays to AI platforms
North American natural-resources M&A hit a record **$330.5bn in 1H26**, utilities/power ~70% of it. NextEra announced an all-stock **$67bn acquisition of Dominion** (combined EV ~$420bn), filed for approval July 15 2026, closing expected 2H 2027. NextEra: 21 GW of hyperscaler opportunities in FPL's pipeline, ~12 GW in advanced negotiation; up to 10 GW of gas generation funded with the US and Japan by end-2028; 15–30 GW of new capacity by 2035; a ~$100bn Brookfield AI campus in Paducah.
- **Action:** NEE is the right anchor but the Dominion deal adds regulatory/execution risk on a stock flat for two years. Anchor, not a grower.

### Nuclear is back — 9.8 GW committed across 13 deals
Amazon, Google, Meta and Microsoft have signed ~**9.8 GW** of nuclear output from operating/restarted/uprated reactors; the IEA counts 45 GW of conditional SMR agreements. First new capacity: Three Mile Island Unit 1's 835 MW restart (Microsoft/Constellation) for H2 2027. Amazon signed a 20-year PPA with Constellation for up to 690 MW from Calvert Cliffs (incl. a 190 MW uprate) plus joint SMR exploration; Meta up to 6.6 GW across TerraPower, Oklo, Vistra and Constellation targeting 2032–2035.
- **Action:** **ADD CEG (3–5%)** — the listed expression of scarce regulated baseload. Not defense; it's power scarcity.

### Behind-the-meter fuel cells — the shortest path to powering an AI campus
Bloom Energy: ~250 MW across nearly two dozen AI customers, from near zero two years ago. Oracle master agreement for **up to 2.8 GW** (1.2 GW contracted). AEP is building a **$4bn, 702 MW Bloom fuel cell plant** near Cheyenne under a 20-year Google PPA, online by end-2028. $5bn Brookfield partnership. Fuel cells can bring a campus online in ~90 days without grid connection.
- **Action:** BE pending order at $248.90 is the highest-risk line in the plan (backtest: +1359% market-aware but −75.9% B&H drawdown). Keep it at 5% max and hold it on the Oracle/AEP contracts, not the backtest.

### US LNG — the structural shock absorber
US LNG exports averaged **17.4 Bcf/d in 1H26, +23% y/y**, the fastest growth since 2016; EIA forecasts 18.7 Bcf/d in 1H27 and ~20% more capacity by end-2027. The March 2026 Hormuz closure cut ~20% of global LNG trade and **doubled US shipments to Asia (+108%)**. JKM hit a four-year high of $15.56/MMBtu.
- **Action:** Hold XOM and CVX. Clearest multi-year trend in the portfolio, and not defense. Don't add — 15% combined is enough, and part of the shock is already in Brent's forward curve ($86 for Dec 2027).

## 4. Space economy

Allianz: LEO services go ~$16bn (2025) → ~$120bn (2030), a 7–8x expansion inside a space economy tripling to $1.8tn+ by 2035.
- **Action:** Don't size RKLB as if launch is the whole space economy.

### Rocket Lab: thesis intact, catalyst moved a year

Stock fell 7.9% on the day, ~7.3% after hours, 6.6% the next session; now ~63% below its May 27 all-time high of $151, closing near $52–53. Fundamentals unchanged and strong: Q2 revenue **$234m (+62% y/y)**, $437m+ new launch contracts, backlog $2.2–2.4bn, 90+ launches, **$397m Space Force SB-AMTI** contract, $160m+ across two GEO satellite builds, a 2028 Kepler dedicated Neutron mission. CFO: cash-flow positivity depends on Neutron's first successful launch and could take 18–24 months after.
- **Action:** Clearest buy-the-delay setup in the book — backlog and awards are real, only the date moved. But $33–35bn market cap against $769m trailing revenue (**~41–54x sales**) on a GAAP loss means small size only. The pending 258-share order at $58.06 is above the current ~$52–53; let it fill and hold. Don't size up until there's a successful flight *plus* 18–24 months of cash-flow commentary.

## 5. Macro

### Rates are going UP, not down
The FOMC unanimously hiked 25bp to **3.75–4.00%** on Sept 15–16, 2026 — first increase since July 2023. September minutes (Oct 7) show officials "overwhelmingly" concluding more work was needed; **16 of 18** policymakers expected at least one more move by year-end, most wanting rates at or above that level through 2027. Fed staff see 2% inflation only in **2029**. October hike odds swung from ~70% to ~23% on Jefferson/Williams pushback, but U.S. Bank now expects **75bp cumulative tightening from Sept 2026 to March 2027, peaking at 4.25–4.50%**.
- **Action:** Most important macro fact for this portfolio. It argues for cutting unprofitable-growth exposure. Sharesies holdings QBTS (−33% in 6 months), RGTI (−35%), IREN (−36%) and DTIL (−35%) are exactly the names a hiking Fed de-rates. **Trim them into power and memory names that earn through the cycle.**

### Inflation is supply-shock driven and the Fed can't fix it
IMF October 2026 WEO (Oct 13, Bangkok): global growth **3.1% (2026), 3.4% (2027)**, inflation reaching **4.7% this year** on energy and food. "The margins of adjustment that made the global economy resilient are rapidly narrowing." IMF: **~30% probability** US growth falls below 1% in 2027 (mild recession from Q4 2026) and **~30% probability** US headline inflation exceeds 4% in 2027. US CPI above 2% for the longest stretch of the 21st century.
- **Action:** **ADD a 3–5% gold sleeve.** Gold is ~25% below its late-Jan 2026 record (spot ~$4,188 on Oct 13); central banks bought **289 tonnes in Q2 2026 (+62% y/y)**; J.P. Morgan still sees $6,000/oz by Q4 2026. On Sharesies: **NZX Smartshares GLD** (PIE-taxed, 0.55% TER) or **ASX GDX**. Highest-conviction addition in this brief.

### China's 15th FYP: technology self-sufficiency, not consumption
Adopted Oct 23, 2025; ratified March 2026. 2026 GDP target cut to **4.5–5%**; binding target to raise total energy production capacity **>20%**; R&D +7%/yr (~40% cumulative); core digital economy to **12.5% of GDP**. Critically, the household-consumption goal stays secondary with **no binding figures or timetable**.
- **Action:** This is the main reason to be cautious on AIR — NZ China-bound goods ran at nearly double the next two export markets combined, so a plan that suppresses Chinese household consumption is a headwind to NZ export income.

### New Zealand: the RBNZ is hiking too
OCR raised 25bp to **2.75%** on Sept 2 with inflation at **4.1%** on Middle East fuel. Chief Economist Conway on Oct 15: the late-October decision is "completely open" between a hold and a hike. ASB expects hikes in **both October and December to a 3.25% end-2026 OCR**; Westpac expects a pause then a December hike, with a **4% peak in 2027 and 4.25% through 2028** before returning to neutral in 2029.
- **Action:** NZ-listed holdings (AIR, CO2, PEB, USH) are the most exposed to a hiking RBNZ. Prefer NZD-hedged or US-listed growth while the OCR is rising — the existing US-heavy allocation is correctly positioned.
